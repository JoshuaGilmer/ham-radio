Ham Radio.

Every pantry a station: broadcast expiring surplus sideways across the pantry network — before the clock runs out.

DEMO

Everything in the running demo is synthetic. Org names are obviously fictional and every number on screen is invented. The only real links on this page are the verified emergency resources at the bottom.

§1 The one person, the one moment

Ray is the volunteer coordinator of a small church food pantry — one of the Community Food Bank of Central Alabama's ~300 partner agencies. He barcodes everything and tracks every expiration date. His problem isn't inventory; it's the gap between when food expires and when his distribution runs.

The decision moment. Monday's inventory check surfaces 120 lbs of food expiring within 72 hours. His distribution is Wednesday — and Wednesday can't absorb it. Today he spends the next hours serially phoning pantries in his personal network: "I've got X, can you take it? What can you send back so the truck isn't empty?"

  • 120 LBS
  • 72-HOUR CLOCK
  • NEXT DISTRIBUTION: WED
  • 8–10 HRS/WK ON THE PHONE

We disclose his geography rather than fudge it. Ray is modeled on a real coordinator in one of our team's families, who runs this exact workflow manually in western North Carolina and cut his pantry's annual food waste from $3–5k to under $500 by doing it. He isn't in Birmingham. Birmingham has roughly 300 structurally identical seats operating under worse conditions — ~3M lbs cut from CFBCA's 2025 supply, ~80,000 Alabamians losing SNAP this month. "One person" is a design discipline, not a residency requirement.

Birmingham's gap is not food or organizations; it's that supply and demand can't see each other in time. FeedBHM already rescues donor surplus into the network. CFBCA's fleet moves food down from the warehouse. Nothing moves food sideways — pantry to pantry — when expiry outruns a distribution schedule. That lateral move is currently one man's phone tree, and Ham Radio replaces it with one asynchronous post.

§3 Guardrail compliance

RuleHow we comply
✓ No static directory Signals auto-expire; the only availability data is self-declared, point-in-time, and self-deleting. Ham Radio structurally cannot rot into a stale directory.
✓ No private client data Org-to-org only. No client ever touches the system; no names, no eligibility records, nothing.
✓ No rationing by algorithm The filter gates physical safety — can you store frozen food, can you get there in time. It never scores need or decides shares. All matches are notified simultaneously; humans claim and confirm every transfer.

✓ Preserve verified emergency links Baked into the failure path — the escalation screen carries verified 211 and CFBCA links.

§4 The two questions we expect

Judge

"You've turned Ray's 8 hours into notification fatigue for eleven other coordinators. How is that not relocating the friction?"

Those eleven coordinators are already fielding Ray's phone calls today — synchronously, unfiltered, mid-task. Ham Radio makes the same signal asynchronous, filtered to food they can physically store, near them, during hours they're open — and ignorable at zero cost, because a signal nobody claims escalates on its own. The friction isn't relocated; it's shrunk and made optional at every node.

Judge

"First-come-first-served is an algorithm allocating food."

The filter gates physical safety — can you store frozen food, can you reach it before it spoils. It never scores need and never decides shares. All compatible orgs are notified at the same moment, and a human confirms both ends of every transfer. Rationing is when software decides who deserves food; Ham Radio only decides who can safely receive a message.
